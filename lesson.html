First aid: bites & stings

Insect bites and stings are one of the most common reasons people first reach for a homeopathic remedy. This lesson covers the handful of remedies worth knowing — what each is for, and how to tell them apart.

Seek emergency care first when needed. Signs of a severe allergic reaction — difficulty breathing, swelling of the lips, tongue, or throat, dizziness, or a spreading rash — are a medical emergency. Call emergency services right away. First-aid remedies are for minor, everyday situations, not a substitute for urgent care.

The remedies to know

Apis mellifica

For stings that are red, swollen, and puffy, with burning, stinging pain that feels better from cold. Think of a bee sting — Apis is made from the honeybee.

Ledum palustre

For puncture wounds and bites where the area feels cold to the touch yet is better for cold applications. A first thought for mosquito and tick bites.

Hypericum

For bites or stings with sharp, shooting, nerve-rich pain — especially on fingertips, toes, or other nerve-dense areas.

How to choose

Match the picture, not just the label. If swelling and puffiness dominate and cold helps, lean Apis. If the wound is a cold-feeling puncture, lean Ledum. If the pain shoots along a nerve, lean Hypericum. With first-aid situations, observation beats memorization — which is the whole spirit of Heilkunst learning.
