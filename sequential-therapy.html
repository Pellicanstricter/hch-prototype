How treatment works

Sequential therapy

The method at the heart of how we treat chronic illness — resolving a lifetime of shocks and traumas in the reverse order they occurred.

Sequential therapy is the treatment of specific shocks and traumas — mainly drugs, vaccines, and surgeries, but also emotional traumas — using energetic (often called "homeopathic") remedies. The traumas are identified by creating a timeline, then treated in reverse order: backwards from the present, to birth and conception, and into the inherited predispositions.

Where it came from

The principle originates with Dr. Samuel Hahnemann (1755–1843). It was developed systematically by Dr. J.F. Elmiger, MD, in Switzerland — who called it la thérapie séquentielle — and brought to North America in the early 1990s by Rudi Verspoor (Canada) and Patty Smith (USA), who added the crucial dimension of emotional shock. Their first book, Homeopathy Renewed, appeared in 1995.

Working in reverse

Imagine peeling back the layers of a life. The most recent shock is treated first, then the next, and the next — each remedy matched to a particular event — until the timeline reaches birth, then the inherited miasms beneath it.

"Sequential Therapy is highly effective — but it is only part of a much broader system of medicine created by Dr. Hahnemann, which he called Heilkunst."

At the Hahnemann Center, sequential therapy is taught within Hahnemann's complete medical system — of which it, and homeopathy, are parts.
